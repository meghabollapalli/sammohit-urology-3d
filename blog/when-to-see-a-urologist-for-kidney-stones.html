Kidney stones: when to see a urologist

22 November 2026Draft · awaiting the doctor’s review

A kidney stone is a hard deposit that forms from minerals in the urine. Many small stones pass on their own. Some need treatment. This article explains how to tell the difference and when to ask for help.

Common symptoms

  • Pain in the side or back that comes in waves and may move towards the groin
  • Blood in the urine, or urine that looks pink or brown
  • Burning when passing urine, or needing to go often
  • Nausea or vomiting with the pain

Go to a hospital the same day if

  • You have fever or chills together with stone pain
  • The pain does not settle with the medicine you were given
  • You cannot pass urine, or you are passing very little
  • You have only one working kidney, or you are pregnant

Fever with a blocked kidney is an emergency. Do not wait for a routine appointment.

What happens at the first visit

The doctor asks about your symptoms and examines you. A urine test, a blood test and a scan (ultrasound or a CT scan) show where the stone is and how big it is. Size and position decide the plan:

  1. Small stones are often given time to pass, with fluids and medicine for pain.
  2. Stones that are stuck or larger may need a procedure, such as laser treatment through a thin telescope.

Bring any earlier scan reports and a list of your medicines.

Before you come

Drink water as usual unless a doctor has told you otherwise. If you pass a stone, keep it in a clean container so it can be tested.
